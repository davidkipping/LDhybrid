# A&A referee brief (mock review)

Condensed from the A&A editorial "Editorship and peer-review at A&A" (Bertout &
Schneider 2004, A&A 420, E1–E14, §4.2), which the current A&A Author guide still
cites as the statement of editorial policy, and from the current Author guide
(aanda.org, read 2026-10-10). Paraphrased; see the sources for the full text.

## What the Editor asks of the referee

Focus on the scientific content. Referees are not expected to correct language or
typography (welcome but optional). The report has five parts:

1. **Scientific content — recommendation** (choose one; A–C recommend eventual
   acceptance, D–F reject):
   - A. acceptable without revision (must be justified, or the Editor seeks a
     second opinion);
   - B. acceptable after minor revision;
   - C. acceptable after major revision;
   - D. not acceptable, but the author should be encouraged to revise
     substantially and resubmit;
   - E. not acceptable: too long for the amount of science presented;
   - F. not acceptable: scientific content insufficient for A&A, or the paper
     contains demonstrable errors.
2. **Style and language**: (a) well written, concise but self-contained;
   (b) could be reduced by xx% without loss of content (estimate realistically
   and say how); (c) would benefit from extensive language editing.
3. **Anonymity**: whether the referee wishes to be identified.
4. **Confidential comments to the Editor**: a frank, balanced assessment of
   strengths and weaknesses; the Editor relies heavily on it.
5. **Detailed scientific report** (sent to the authors; factual and courteous,
   never condescending), considering at least:
   1. Why should this paper be published? (relevance, usefulness to the
      community, validity — the most important question)
   2. Are the assumptions spelled out clearly?
   3. Are the methods fully described?
   4. Are the new results adequately emphasised?
   5. Are all the figures and tables necessary and properly laid out?
   6. Which material should be published in electronic form only (appendices,
      CDS)?
   7. Is the designation of objects according to IAU rules?

## Current A&A policies relevant to the review

- Regular papers present new results or ideas of sufficient interest "as
  concisely as possible"; the main body is limited to 12 pages (from April 2025).
- Information content matters as well as quality; catalogues and theoretical
  tables of lasting value are welcome, archived at the CDS with a ReadMe.
- Data needed to reproduce the results must be published (usually CDS).
- Structured abstracts (Context / Aims / Methods / Results / Conclusions).
- Figures only when necessary; most should be one column wide; the scientific
  discussion belongs in the text, not the captions; every line style and symbol
  must be explained in the legend.
- Proper attribution: papers must cite directly relevant prior work; references
  to work "in preparation" are not evidence a referee can evaluate.

## Format of the mock report

```
Manuscript: Limb-darkening coefficients for rational laws with elementary transit light curves
Referee: <persona label>
Recommendation (A&A grade): <A–F>   [= Accept / Minor revision / Moderate revision / Major revision / Reject]
Style and language: <a/b/c, with % and how if b>
Identify referee: <yes/no>

Confidential comments to the Editor
...

Report to the authors
Summary of the paper
General assessment (Q1–Q7)
Major comments (numbered)
Minor comments (numbered, with section/equation/figure/table references)
```

"Moderate revision" is not an A&A category; use it only as a gloss between B and
C if helpful, and always give the A&A grade.
